# People Manager Guide

**Calloway Ridge Credit Union, Human Resources**
Edition: 2026.1 (published March 2026)
Prepared by Casey Wren, Senior HR Business Partner

This guide is for anyone who manages people at Calloway Ridge, from branch managers to department heads. It covers the things new managers ask HR most often. It does not replace the policies; it tells you what they mean for you in practice.

---

## Part 1: Your first 30 days as a manager

- Meet each of your direct reports one to one within your first two weeks. Ask what they need from you, not just what they are working on.
- Review each person's job description, last performance review, and any active development plan.
- Check your Harbor approval queues: time corrections, PTO requests, remote work requests.
- Check your Clearline approval queue. Reports waiting on you delay someone's reimbursement.
- Complete the Manager Essentials e-learning (three modules, about 90 minutes total).

## Part 2: Scheduling, time and attendance

Non-exempt employees must be paid for all time worked. Do not ask anyone to "finish up off the clock." If someone regularly works through lunch, fix the workload.

Approve or reject PTO requests within three business days. You can decline a request for coverage reasons, but be consistent, and suggest alternative dates.

Attendance problems: talk to the employee early, document the conversation in Harbor, and loop in your HR Business Partner before any written warning.

## Part 3: Expenses and approvals

You are the first line of control on spending. When you approve a report in Clearline, you are confirming that the expense was for business, was reasonable, and follows policy.

What to check:

1. Is there a business purpose for every line?
2. Is the hotel within the nightly cap, or is there an approval attached?
3. Are there receipts where required?
4. Is the per diem calculated correctly for first and last days?
5. Is mileage reasonable for the trip described?

**Deadlines.** Remind your team that expense reports should be submitted promptly. Employees have 60 days from the end of a trip to submit their report in Clearline; after that, Finance will return the report unless the CFO grants an exception.

**Approval routing.** Clearline sends each report to the right people based on its total, using the L1, L2 and L3 approval levels Finance Operations sets up. As a direct manager you are L1 for your team. Department heads approve at L2 and can sign off on anything up to the L2 ceiling without involving the CFO. The CFO only sees reports above that.

For context: until the end of 2025, a direct manager could approve reports up to $750 alone. That limit no longer applies.

**Conflicts.** Never approve a report for a trip you were on. Reassign it to your own manager in Clearline.

## Part 4: Remote and hybrid work

Remote Work Requests come to you in Harbor first, then to HR. Before you approve:

- Confirm the employee has finished the 90-day introductory period.
- Confirm the role is eligible in the job catalog.
- Agree the in-office days for hybrid arrangements (at least two a week, or more if your department requires it).
- Make sure the home address in Harbor is in Kendry or Ossary.

If you need to change or end someone's arrangement for business reasons, talk to your HR Business Partner first. The employee must get at least 30 days' written notice, and HR will help you draft the letter. Shorter notice is only allowed for documented performance or security issues.

Treat remote and on-site employees the same when it comes to assignments, recognition and promotion. Out of sight must not mean out of mind.

## Part 5: Performance

Reviews happen once a year in February, with a mid-year check-in in August. Ratings: Exceeds, Meets, Partially Meets, Does Not Meet.

A performance improvement plan (PIP) lasts 60 or 90 days. Talk to HR before starting one. An employee on a PIP cannot start a new remote arrangement.

## Part 6: When someone leaves

- Accept the resignation in writing and forward it to HR the same day.
- Remind the employee to return their equipment. IT sends the box.
- Complete the exit checklist in Harbor so system access is removed on the last day.
- Do not promise anything about references; direct reference requests to HR.

## Part 7: Getting help

Your HR Business Partner is your first call for anything involving discipline, accommodations, leave, complaints or terminations. Do not investigate complaints on your own.
