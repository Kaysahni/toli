# Contractor Engagement Guide

**For hiring managers who bring in independent contractors and consultants**
Owner: Procurement and Vendor Management, with HR
Version 3, April 2026

## 1. Who this guide is for

Use this guide when you engage an independent contractor or consulting firm, for example a core banking implementation consultant, a freelance designer, or an interim project manager. It does not apply to employees, including part-time and seasonal employees, and it does not apply to agency temporary staff.

Contractors are not employees. Employee policies, including the Travel and Business Expense Policy (HR-214) and the Remote and Hybrid Work Policy (HR-220), do not apply to them. Their terms come from the signed engagement agreement.

## 2. Before you engage

1. Confirm with HR that the work is genuinely contractor work (the misclassification checklist is in Appendix 1).
2. Get at least two quotes for engagements above $25,000.
3. Vendor Management runs a risk review. Anyone who will access member data needs a background check and a signed confidentiality agreement.
4. The engagement agreement must be signed before work begins.

## 3. Where contractors work

Contractors decide where and how they do the work, within the security requirements of their agreement. Contractors who need network access use a Credit Union laptop issued by IT under a loan agreement, and return it within 5 business days of the engagement ending. Contractors do not receive a connectivity stipend or home office allowance.

## 4. Contractor expenses

Our standard engagement agreement is fixed fee or hourly, with expenses included in the rate. Where an agreement allows separate reimbursement of travel expenses, these are the standard contractor terms:

| Item | Standard contractor terms |
|---|---|
| Meals | Actual cost with receipts, up to $70 per day |
| Lodging | Actual cost, up to $225 per night |
| Mileage | 55 cents per mile |
| Receipts | Required for every expense over $75 |
| Submission | Expenses invoiced monthly, no later than 60 days after they are incurred |
| Approval | Engagement owner approves invoices up to $5,000; Procurement approves above that |

Contractor expenses are invoiced to Accounts Payable. They are never entered in Clearline.

## 5. Managing the engagement

- Do not supervise contractors like employees: no set hours, no inclusion in team schedules, no performance reviews.
- Review deliverables against the statement of work.
- Contractors cannot approve anything in Credit Union systems.

## 6. Ending the engagement

Follow the termination clause in the agreement (most of ours allow either side to end with 15 days' written notice). Tell IT the same day so access can be removed.

## Appendix 1: Misclassification checklist

A worker is more likely an employee than a contractor if we control how, when and where the work is done; provide the tools; pay by the hour for open-ended work; or the work is a core, ongoing part of our business. If three or more of these apply, talk to HR before engaging.
