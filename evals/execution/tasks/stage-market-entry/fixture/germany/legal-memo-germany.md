Re: Launch of Varnholt consumer and small-business note-taking service in Germany

1. Questions presented

You asked us to advise on (a) whether German law requires Varnholt to store German users' content in Germany, (b) consumer law issues for monthly subscriptions, and (c) registration and representative requirements.

2. Short answers

(a) No. As of the date of this memo, there is no German statute or regulation that requires a provider of a note-taking or document storage service for consumers and small businesses to store user content on servers located in Germany. Storage in Varnholt's existing EU hosting region is permissible, subject to the general data protection framework that applies across the EU and which Varnholt already complies with for its Netherlands launch.

(b) German consumer law requires a clear cancellation button for online subscriptions, a specific confirmation page before payment, and that auto-renewing contracts can be cancelled monthly after the first term. Varnholt's current checkout meets the first two. The third requires a change to annual plans only (not in scope for monthly Plus).

(c) Varnholt does not need a German entity to sell to German consumers. If Varnholt chooses to form one for tax or hiring reasons, trade registration is routine. No local representative is required because Varnholt has an establishment in the EU (Amsterdam).

3. Discussion

3.1 Data storage location

We reviewed federal law, the laws of the three states where Varnholt expects most early users (Berlin, Bavaria, North Rhine-Westphalia), and sector rules. Location rules exist in Germany for certain public-sector data, some health records held by providers, and tax records held by businesses about their own bookkeeping. None of these applies to a general-purpose note-taking service offered to the public. A user who chooses to put health or tax information into their notes does not change this analysis, because the obligation rests on the regulated entity (the clinic, the business), not on the software vendor.

We are aware of discussion in the Bundestag digital committee about "sovereign cloud" requirements for commercial providers. At the date of this memo, no draft has been published. We will monitor it.
