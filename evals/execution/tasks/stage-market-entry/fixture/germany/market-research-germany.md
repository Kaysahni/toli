# Varnholt: Germany Market Opportunity Assessment

Prepared by Feldhaus & Brandl Marktforschung for Varnholt Growth
Final version, 14 May 2026

---

## 1. Summary

Germany is a large, mature, paying market for productivity software. Consumers and small businesses are used to paying monthly for tools they rely on, card and SEPA direct debit penetration is high, and there is visible dissatisfaction with the two established local note-taking apps, mainly around sync reliability and dated design.

We see a credible path to a mid-sized paid base within twelve months of launch, provided Varnholt ships a complete German localization (app, help center, billing emails) and supports SEPA direct debit from day one. We do not recommend launching with English-only support content. German reviewers are unforgiving about half-translated products, and we found three app store reviews for a competitor that mention "Denglisch" in the first sentence.

Headline projection (base case): **4,300 paying Plus subscribers at month 12**, at a tested price of **EUR 7.49 per month (US$8.10 at the June planning rate)**.

## 2. Method

- 22 in-depth interviews (students in Berlin, Leipzig and Munich; freelancers; owners of small agencies and practices)
- Desk review of app store rankings, category spend data and competitor pricing
- Input from Varnholt's own wave 3 pricing survey was not available when this report was finalized; our segment views below are based on interviews and should be checked against it
- Funnel model built from Varnholt's Canada and Netherlands launch data, adjusted for German price sensitivity

## 3. Market size and willingness to pay

Germany has roughly 2.9 million people who use a dedicated note-taking app at least weekly (our estimate, triangulated from category downloads and a 2025 consumer panel). About 31% of them pay for some productivity subscription already, which is well above the European average.

Price sensitivity sits in a narrow band. Interviewees anchored on EUR 5 to EUR 9 per month for a personal plan. Above EUR 9 the conversation turned to "then I'd rather use the office suite I already have." At EUR 7.49 nobody rejected the price outright.

Annual plans are popular in Germany, but per Varnholt's instructions we modeled monthly pricing only.

## 4. Segments

**Students.** Strong fit. Lecture notes, shared notebooks for study groups, and PDF annotation came up repeatedly. Students are price sensitive but used to student discounts; the tested price was accepted by most without a discount.

**Independent professionals.** Strong fit. Freelance translators, consultants and therapists want reliable offline access and clean export. Several mentioned that client confidentiality makes them wary of US-hosted tools, although none said this would stop them buying.

**Small teams.** In our view also reachable. Small agencies and practices liked shared workspaces. Budget holders were slower to commit and wanted invoices with a VAT line, which Varnholt already supports.

We consider all three segments reachable in Germany. We expect the survey to confirm this, though small teams are the least certain of the three.

## 5. Competitive landscape

Direct competitors (dedicated note-taking apps):

| App | Positioning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Notiztafel | Market leader, older user base | Paid plans only, 14-day trial. Frequent complaints about sync. |
| Blattwerk | Design-led, popular with students | Paid plans only. Strong on handwriting. |

Adjacent tools that people also use for notes: the notes modules inside large office suites, and chat tools with pinned message features. These are not dedicated note apps and we have not modeled them as direct competition.

Separate one-page profiles of Notiztafel and Blattwerk are in the competitors folder.

## 6. Funnel model

| Stage | Month 12 (base case) |
|---|---|
| Registered users (cumulative) | 61,000 |
| Monthly active users | 27,500 |
| Paying Plus subscribers | 4,300 |
| Monthly price (USD) | 8.10 |

Assumptions: organic plus a modest launch campaign, conversion from active to paid of 15.6% by month 12 (Netherlands reached 17.1% but had a lower price point), monthly churn of 3.2%.

## 7. Downside case (macro)

If German consumer confidence weakens further in the second half of 2026, we would expect slower paid conversion among students and freelancers. In that case we project **3,600 paying subscribers at month 12**. We include this for context. We do not think it is the most likely outcome.

## 8. Regulatory notes

Varnholt's counsel reviewed data protection and hosting questions separately. Our understanding from the March legal memo is that storing German users' notes in Varnholt's existing EU region is sufficient. We have not assessed this independently and defer to counsel.

## 9. Go-to-market recommendations

1. Launch with full German localization including the help center.
2. SEPA direct debit at launch, invoices with VAT line for teams.
3. Seed with university study groups in the first semester after launch (October intake is ideal).
4. Target freelancer communities through professional associations and coworking spaces.
5. Treat sync reliability as the core message; it is the most common pain point with incumbents.

## 10. Risks

- Notiztafel may respond with a price cut. It has done so once before, in 2023, when a US competitor entered.
- German reviewers weight privacy messaging heavily. Any incident would be costly.
- The tested price assumes no student discount. A discount would lower ARPU.
