Varnholt Japan Entry Study
==========================

Kasumi Strategy Partners K.K., Tokyo
Report date: 20 May 2026
Engagement lead: Haruto Nishimura | Team: Aoi Fujisawa, Kenji Brennan-Oda

------------------------------------------------------------

1. Key conclusions
------------------

1.1 Japan is the most valuable of the markets we have studied for Varnholt this year on a per-subscriber basis. Willingness to pay for well-made productivity software is high and churn is low once a user has built up a notebook library.

1.2 The price point we tested, JPY 1,080 per month, was well received. At the June planning rate supplied by Varnholt Finance this is US$7.20.

1.3 Base case: 5,200 paying Plus subscribers at month 12.

1.4 The main risk is not price. It is the possibility that the local leader, Kumonote, responds to a new entrant by introducing a free plan. Kumonote has a large base of lapsed trial users that a free plan would reactivate. We model this in section 6.

1.5 Localization quality is decisive. Machine-translated UI text is noticed immediately and punished in reviews.

2. Approach
-----------

- 30 interviews (Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka), including 8 with small company owners
- Diary study, 12 participants, 2 weeks
- Review analysis of the top 10 note apps in the Japanese App Store and Google Play
- Funnel model calibrated on Varnholt's Korea beta data, with Japanese churn benchmarks

Varnholt's own pricing survey was not complete when we finished; we understand it will be used for the segment decision.

3. Market
---------

Roughly 3.4 million people in Japan use a dedicated note-taking app weekly. Paid penetration among them is about 22%. Japanese users are more likely than European users to pay for a single app they love rather than rely on bundled tools, and less likely to switch once settled, which makes the first year of acquisition expensive but the long tail valuable.

Handwriting input and vertical text are not requirements for most users, but correct input method (IME) behavior is. We saw two diary participants abandon a competitor after IME conversion glitches.

4. Segments (interview view)
----------------------------

Students
  In interviews, students were the most excited group. We expect students to be the easiest segment to reach, especially through university co-op stores and study communities. Caveat: several students assumed their parents would pay, so interview enthusiasm may overstate paid conversion.

Independent professionals
  Strong. Freelance designers, translators and writers were willing to pay immediately if export and offline work well.

Small teams
  Strong. Small offices currently share notes through spreadsheet files and chat. Owners liked shared notebooks with simple permissions. Invoice payment (bank transfer) is expected.

5. Competitors
--------------

Direct (dedicated note-taking apps):

  Kumonote     Clear market leader. Paid plans only today (see profile).
               Very large trial funnel, high brand awareness.
  Fudebako     Smaller, handwriting-first, popular with illustrators.
               Paid only, one-time purchase plus optional sync subscription.

Adjacent (not dedicated note apps):

  Shiori Works Office and collaboration suite with a notes module.
               Has a free plan. Used by small offices mainly for
               documents and chat. We do not treat it as a direct competitor.

6. Funnel model and scenarios
-----------------------------

                                     Base case    Free-plan scenario
  Registered users, month 12           58,000          52,000
  Monthly active users, month 12       29,000          26,000
  Paying Plus subscribers, month 12     5,200           3,000
  Price, USD per month                   7.20            7.20

The free-plan scenario assumes a direct competitor offers a permanent free plan with unlimited notes and sync on all devices during Varnholt's first year. In that case many would-be switchers stay on the competitor's free plan, and Varnholt's own free-to-paid conversion falls because the free alternative is "good enough."

We would put the probability of Kumonote launching a free plan within 12 months at around 30%, based on its hiring and on comments by its founder at a conference in April.

7. Other risk: yen weakness
---------------------------

A further 10% fall in the yen would reduce USD revenue proportionally. We have not built a separate scenario for this, as Finance asked agencies to use the planning rate.

8. Recommendations
------------------

  a) Invest in native Japanese copywriting, not only translation.
  b) Support konbini payment and bank transfer invoices for teams.
  c) Launch in April (new fiscal and academic year) if possible.
  d) Prepare a response plan for a Kumonote free plan before launch.
