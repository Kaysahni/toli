SUBJECT:  Japan: data location, cross-border transfer, and consumer rules for Varnholt

I. SUMMARY

1. Japanese law does not require Varnholt to store Japanese users' content in Japan. Varnholt may continue to use its US and EU hosting.

2. Transferring personal information about users in Japan to servers outside Japan is permitted, but Varnholt must either obtain the user's consent to the foreign transfer after informing them of the destination country and its data protection system, or rely on the contractual and organizational safeguards route. For a consumer app, the consent route is simpler. This is a disclosure and consent obligation. It does not restrict where data is stored.

3. Consumer subscription rules require a final confirmation screen before purchase showing price, renewal and cancellation terms. Varnholt's current flow needs minor changes.

4. No local entity or representative is required to offer the service. A Japanese-language contact point for privacy inquiries is required.

II. DATA LOCATION

We reviewed the national personal information protection statute and its guidelines, the telecommunications business rules as they apply to app providers, and sector guidance. Location requirements in Japan are limited to specific public-sector systems and certain regulated sectors (for example, some financial and medical record systems operated by regulated institutions). A general-purpose note-taking application is not within any of them.

Registration as a telecommunications business may be required for services that relay communications between users. Varnholt's shared notebook feature does not, in our view, amount to relaying communications, but if Varnholt adds messaging we should revisit this. Registration, where required, would not impose storage location obligations.

We are not aware of any pending bill or government plan that would introduce a general data location requirement for consumer software providers.
