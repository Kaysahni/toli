title: Paying bonuses
author: Sunniva Hart

Bonuses can be paid two ways in Halvard: added to a regular payroll, or as their own off-cycle payroll. Both are fine. This article explains the difference and walks through each.

## Option A: add the bonus to a regular payroll

On the Run payroll page, click **Add earnings** next to a team member and choose **Bonus**. Enter the amount. The bonus is taxed together with their regular wages for that pay period.

This is the simplest option and it's what most small businesses do for holiday bonuses.

## Option B: pay the bonus in its own off-cycle payroll

Choose this when you want the bonus to arrive on a different day from regular pay, or you want it taxed separately with supplemental withholding. See *Run an off-cycle payroll* for the full steps; choose **Bonus** as the reason.

## Side by side

| | Regular payroll | Off-cycle payroll |
|---|---|---|
| When it's paid | On the next regular pay date | Any pay date you choose (two banking days minimum for direct deposit) |
| Withholding | Combined with regular wages | Supplemental rate by default |
| Benefits deductions | Taken as usual | Skipped by default |
| Evergreen Retirement contributions | Taken as usual | Your choice per run |
| What happens after you click Submit | Goes straight to processing | Goes straight to processing |
| Shows on pay stub as | A Bonus line on the regular stub | A separate stub |

## Gross up a bonus

If you want the employee to take home a specific amount, use **Gross up** on the amount field and enter the net figure. Halvard works out the gross amount needed after taxes. Gross up is available for both options.

## Frequently asked

**Is a gift card a bonus?**
Yes, for tax purposes gift cards are treated as wages. Record them with the **Non-cash bonus** earning type so taxes are withheld without paying cash.

**Can I pay bonuses to contractors?**
Pay contractors through a contractor payment instead. Contractor payments don't use earning types or withholding.

**My bonus shows up as a separate earning type in reports. Why?**
Bonus is its own earning type, so reports list it separately from regular wages. That's expected.
