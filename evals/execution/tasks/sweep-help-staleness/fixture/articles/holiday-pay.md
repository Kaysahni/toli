There's no single rule that says you must pay extra for holidays. Most of it is your company policy. Halvard gives you a few ways to handle it, and the right one depends on what you've promised your team.

## Paid holidays for salaried employees

Nothing to do. Salaried employees get their normal salary whether or not the office is closed.

## Paid holidays for hourly employees

If hourly staff get paid for a day off:

1. Go to **Time off > Policies** and create a policy called something like "Company holiday."
2. Set it to **Unlimited** and **No approval needed**.
3. On the payroll draft, add the holiday hours under that policy for each hourly person.

Using a time off policy (rather than just adding regular hours) keeps your hours worked accurate, which matters for overtime. Holiday hours aren't hours worked, so they don't count toward the overtime threshold.

## Holiday premium for people who work

If you pay extra for working on a holiday, for example time and a half, add an earning:

- **Payroll > Earnings > Add earning**
- Type: Holiday premium
- Rate: multiplier, 1.5

On the draft, enter holiday hours worked in the Holiday premium column. We pay those hours at 1.5 times the regular rate.

Note: Holiday premium hours are hours worked, so they do count toward overtime. If someone works 44 hours in a week and 8 of those were on a holiday, you may owe both the holiday premium and overtime, depending on your policy. Some companies pay only the higher of the two. Halvard pays both by default; switch to "higher of" under Settings > Payroll policies.

## Floating holidays

Create a time off policy with a fixed yearly balance, like 16 hours, that resets on January 1.

## Questions we get a lot

**Do I have to give holidays off?** Private employers in Delmora and North Carrow generally don't. Check your own state and any union agreement.

**Can different teams have different holidays?** Yes. Create separate policies and assign them by department.
