# Pre-tax benefits deductions

Last updated: February 4, 2026
Author: Rhiannon Castellanos, Support Content

Pre-tax deductions come out of an employee's pay before income taxes are calculated. That lowers their taxable wages, which means they pay less in tax. Health insurance premiums, HSA contributions and commuter benefits are the most common examples.

This article explains how pre-tax deductions work in Halvard, how to set them up if you manage benefits outside Halvard, and what to check when something looks off.

---

## Which deductions can be pre-tax?

| Deduction type | Reduces federal income tax | Reduces social insurance taxes | Reduces state income tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical, dental, vision premiums (section 125 plan) | Yes | Yes | Yes, in Delmora and North Carrow |
| HSA (through a section 125 plan) | Yes | Yes | Yes in Delmora, no in North Carrow |
| Health care FSA | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Dependent care FSA | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Commuter transit and parking | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Traditional retirement contributions | Yes | No | Yes |
| Roth retirement contributions | No | No | No |

If you're not sure whether your health plan is set up as a section 125 plan, ask your broker. Without one, premiums have to be deducted after tax.

## If your benefits are managed in Halvard

You don't need to do anything. When an employee enrolls through **Benefits**, the right deductions are created automatically with the right tax treatment.

## If your benefits are managed somewhere else

You'll add deductions by hand.

1. Go to **Team > Directory** and open the employee.
2. Select the **Deductions** tab.
3. Click **Add deduction** and pick the deduction type from the list. The type controls the tax treatment, so pick carefully. "Medical (pre-tax)" and "Medical (post-tax)" are different entries.
4. Enter the employee amount per paycheck. If you contribute too, enter the company amount.
5. Set the start date. Deductions only apply to payrolls with a pay date on or after this date.
6. Save.

To add the same deduction to many people at once, use **Benefits > Bulk deductions** and upload a spreadsheet with one row per employee.

> **Tip:** If an employee's premium changes mid-year, don't delete the old deduction. Set an end date on it and add a new one. That keeps the history accurate for year-end.

## Company contributions

Company contributions to health premiums are generally not taxable to the employee and don't show up in their gross pay. They do appear on the pay stub under "Company contributions" so employees can see the full value of their benefits.

HSA contributions from the company count toward the employee's annual HSA limit. Halvard tracks the combined total and stops deducting when the limit is reached. See "HSA contributions" for details.

## Troubleshooting

**The employee's taxes didn't go down after adding a deduction.** Check the deduction type. If it says "post-tax", it won't reduce taxable wages. You can't change the type on an existing deduction; end it and create a new one.

**A deduction didn't come out of a paycheck.** Look at the start date, and check whether the employee's net pay was too low to cover it. Halvard skips deductions that would push net pay below zero and shows a warning in the payroll preview.

**An employee wants a refund of a deduction taken by mistake.** Add a one-time reimbursement on their next regular payroll, or run an off-cycle payroll (see "Run an off-cycle payroll"). Talk to your benefits provider first, since they may have already received the money.

**The tax treatment in Delmora looks different from North Carrow.** That's expected. The two states treat HSA contributions differently, as shown in the table above.
