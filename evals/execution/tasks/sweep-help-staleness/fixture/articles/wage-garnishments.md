Wage garnishments
=================

Updated 2026-03-02 by Joanna Pruitt

A garnishment is a court or agency order that tells you, the employer, to withhold part of an employee's pay and send it to someone else. Child support is the most common kind. Others include creditor judgments, student loan orders and unpaid state taxes.

Halvard can calculate and withhold garnishments for you. Depending on the type, we can also send the payment to the agency on your behalf.

Adding a garnishment
--------------------

1. Open the employee's profile from Team > Directory.
2. Go to the Deductions tab and click Add deduction.
3. Choose Garnishment, then the garnishment type.
4. Enter the details from the order: case number, issuing agency, amount or percentage, and any maximum total.
5. Upload a copy of the order. You'll need it if the employee has questions later.
6. Choose whether Halvard pays the agency or you pay it yourself.
7. Save.

Note: If you receive more than one order for the same employee, add each one separately. Halvard applies them in priority order (support orders first) and respects the limits on how much of a paycheck can be garnished in total.

How much gets withheld
----------------------

For percentage-based orders, the percentage is applied to disposable earnings, which is gross pay minus taxes and certain required deductions. Voluntary deductions like retirement contributions or HSA contributions are not subtracted when calculating disposable earnings.

If the calculated amount would push the employee over the legal limit, Halvard withholds the maximum allowed and shows a note on the payroll preview. You don't need to do anything, but it's worth letting the agency know if the order can't be fully satisfied.

When Halvard pays the agency
----------------------------

For child support orders in Delmora and North Carrow, Halvard sends payment electronically to the state disbursement unit after each payroll. For other orders, we mail a check with the case number printed on the memo line.

Payments show up in the Garnishment payments report under Reports.

Stopping a garnishment
----------------------

Only stop a garnishment when you have a release letter or the order says the balance is paid. Open the deduction, set an end date, and attach the release.

If an employee disputes a garnishment, keep withholding until you receive something in writing from the issuing agency. We can't advise on individual disputes, but we're happy to show you where the records are.
