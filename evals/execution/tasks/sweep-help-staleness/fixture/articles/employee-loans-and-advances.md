# Employee loans and pay advances

Updated 2026-02-03. Maintained by Calla Nyström.

**Q: Does Halvard lend money to employees?**
No. Halvard doesn't provide loans. You can lend money to an employee yourself and use Halvard to collect repayments from their paychecks.

**Q: How do I set up repayment deductions?**
Open **Team > Directory**, pick the employee, go to **Deductions**, and click **Add deduction**. Choose **Loan repayment**. Enter the total amount owed and either a per-paycheck amount or a number of paychecks. Halvard stops the deduction automatically once the balance reaches zero.

**Q: Can I pay the advance itself through Halvard?**
Yes. Add it as a **Pay advance** earning on a regular payroll, or run an off-cycle payroll if you need to pay it sooner (see **Run an off-cycle payroll**). A pay advance isn't taxed when it's paid, because it's an advance on wages the employee will earn later. Taxes are applied when the wages are earned.

**Q: What about a true loan, not an advance?**
Pay the loan outside of payroll (for example, a bank transfer) and only use Halvard for the repayment deduction. Loans aren't wages, so they shouldn't go on a pay stub.

**Q: Can a repayment take someone below minimum wage?**
Halvard warns you if a deduction would bring an hourly employee's pay below the minimum wage for their work location. Some states don't allow that at all. Delmora, for one, requires written consent from the employee for any loan deduction, and it still can't cut into minimum wage.

**Q: The employee left before paying it back. Now what?**
The deduction stops when the employee is moved to **Team > Past members**. Collecting the rest is between you and the former employee.

Note: keep a signed repayment agreement in the employee's documents. Halvard can store it under **Documents**.
