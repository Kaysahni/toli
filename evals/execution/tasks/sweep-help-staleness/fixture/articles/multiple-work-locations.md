Title: Employees who work in more than one location
Author: Gwen Farrow-Ibe
Last updated: 2026-02-17

Short version: set a primary work location on each employee, and add extra locations only when someone genuinely splits their time.

---

WHY LOCATION MATTERS

Where someone works decides which state and local taxes apply, which minimum wage applies, and in some places whether they earn paid sick leave. Home address matters too (for resident taxes), but work location drives most of it.

ADDING A LOCATION TO YOUR COMPANY

Settings > Locations > Add location. Enter the street address. We look up which state, county and city tax jurisdictions apply. If we find a jurisdiction you aren't registered in yet, you'll see a banner with a link to the registration steps.

ASSIGNING A PRIMARY LOCATION

Team > Directory > (employee) > Job > Work location. Every employee needs one.

EMPLOYEES WHO SPLIT TIME

On the Job tab, click "Add another location." You then have two options:

  Option A: Fixed split
    Enter a percentage for each location, for example 60% Delmora warehouse, 40% North Carrow office. Each payroll, wages are allocated using those percentages.

  Option B: Per-payroll hours
    Leave the split blank and enter hours by location on each payroll draft. Best for people whose schedule changes week to week.

Screenshot: the Job tab with two work locations and a 60/40 fixed split.

REMOTE EMPLOYEES

Set the work location to the employee's home address and mark it as "Remote." Don't use your office address for someone who never goes there; that sends taxes to the wrong place.

MARREN COUNTY NOTE

Marren County has its own occupational tax for people who work inside the county. If you add a location there, we'll ask for your county account number before the next payroll.

TRAVELLING EMPLOYEES

A salesperson who visits clients across several states for a day or two at a time usually keeps a single primary location. Talk to your accountant if someone spends significant stretches working in another state.
