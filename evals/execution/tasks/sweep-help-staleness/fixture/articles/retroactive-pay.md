Retroactive pay
===============

Author: Samira Keddie | Updated: 2025-03-27

Retro pay covers wages you should have paid in an earlier pay period but didn't. The usual cause is a raise that was approved late: the new rate took effect on the 1st, but payroll for that period already went out at the old rate.

When to use it
--------------
- A raise with an effective date in the past
- An employee who was paid at the wrong rate by mistake
- Shift differentials that were left off a previous payroll

Don't use retro pay for missed hours. If someone worked hours that weren't paid, add those hours to the current payroll or run an off-cycle payroll (see "Run an off-cycle payroll").

Letting Halvard calculate it
----------------------------
When you change a pay rate and pick an effective date in the past, Halvard asks: "Calculate retro pay for affected pay periods?" Click **Yes** and we:

1. Find every payroll between the effective date and today.
2. Recalculate gross pay for the employee using the new rate, including overtime at the new regular rate.
3. Add the difference to the next regular payroll as a "Retro pay" earning.

You'll see a breakdown in the payroll draft: which pay periods, how many hours, and the difference for each.

Entering it yourself
--------------------
If you'd rather calculate it yourself, add a **Retro pay** earning on the payroll draft and enter the amount. Use the memo field to note which periods it covers so the employee understands their pay stub.

Taxes
-----
Retro pay is taxed as supplemental wages in the period it's paid, not the period it was earned. That's normal and expected.

Note: Retirement contributions are calculated on retro pay if the employee's Evergreen Retirement election includes all earnings. Check the plan settings if you're not sure.
