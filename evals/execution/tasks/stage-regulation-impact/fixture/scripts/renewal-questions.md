# Renewal Questions (Customer Care)

Owner: Customer Care Enablement (Tobias Nwachukwu) | Last update: 2026-03-02

Applies to Norvale and Kessington personal lines callers. For Kessington specific rules, see the Kessington renewal notes (separate file).

---

**Caller asks why their premium went up**

1. Pull up the renewal in PolicyCenter and open the Rating Factors comparison.
2. Explain the top changes in everyday words. Examples:
   - "The cost to rebuild homes like yours has gone up, so your dwelling coverage went up with it."
   - "There was a claim on the policy in the last three years."
   - "Rates for all Crestline policies in the state went up this year."
3. Offer ways to lower it: higher deductible, roof or alarm discounts, bundling.
4. If the caller wants to shop, be respectful. Offer to connect them with their agent.

Never say "it's just the computer" or "everyone's premium went up".

**Caller asks to change their coverage before renewal**

Changes that reduce coverage (lower dwelling limit, remove an endorsement) need agent involvement. Create a service request for the agent. If the caller wants to lower Coverage A below our replacement cost estimate, explain that the 80% rule in the loss settlement condition may mean they are not paid in full after a loss.

**Caller received a nonrenewal letter**

1. Express empathy.
2. Read the reason code from the Underwriting tab.
3. Do not promise the decision will change. If the caller believes the information we used is wrong (for example, a roof age that is out of date), open an Underwriting Reconsideration task and tell the caller underwriting will contact them within 5 business days.
4. Suggest they contact their agent now about replacement coverage.

**Caller asks to pay the renewal in installments**

Explain the plans: annual (no fee), two pay ($3 fee per installment), monthly automatic draft ($2 per installment). Card payments over $2,000 are declined by the processor; suggest bank draft.

**Caller wants their renewal documents emailed**

Check eDelivery consent. If no consent, the caller can opt in by phone. Read the consent statement word for word, then record consent in PolicyCenter.
