# Module 6: Claims Basics for Agents

_Agency Development with Claims Operations. Updated November 2025._

## Your role in a claim

You are often the first person a customer calls. Your job is to make them feel looked after and get the claim into our system quickly. You are **not** the one who decides coverage, and you should avoid saying anything that sounds like a coverage promise ("that'll definitely be covered").

## Reporting a claim for a customer

Use the Agency Portal > Report a Loss form. Fill in everything you can. The claim number appears on the confirmation screen; give it to the customer right away.

If you're not sure whether something is covered, report it anyway. It costs the customer nothing to open a claim and it protects their rights under the "prompt notice" condition.

## What happens after

Walk the customer through what to expect:

- They get a letter confirming the claim.
- An adjuster is assigned and calls them.
- For larger losses, a field adjuster inspects. For smaller ones, a desk adjuster may settle using photos.
- Payment follows agreement on the amount.

## Things customers ask you

**"Will my premium go up?"** Possibly. Explain that one claim does not automatically lead to a nonrenewal, but claims history is a rating factor.

**"Can I use my own contractor?"** Yes. We may still send our own estimator.

**"They denied my claim, can you fix it?"** You can't overturn a decision, but you can help the customer understand the letter and make sure any new information reaches the adjuster. The letter explains how to ask for a review.

## Catastrophes

During a declared CAT, expect longer waits. Encourage customers to report through the portal and to upload photos early.

## Do not

- Do not tell a customer to start permanent repairs before the adjuster has seen the damage (temporary repairs to prevent more damage are fine and expected).
- Do not recommend a public adjuster or contractor by name.
- Do not accept payment of a deductible on our behalf.
