Three years ago this week, four of us shipped the first version of Quillmark from a borrowed office above a bike shop. It had one chart type (a line chart, and not a great one), a single database connector, and a login page that our designer still refuses to talk about.

I want to use this post to say thank you, and to be honest about a few things we got wrong.

## What we got right

We bet early that most teams did not want another BI suite. They wanted a place where the numbers they already argued about in meetings would sit still long enough to be discussed. Almost every decision we made since then came back to that: fast dashboards, boring reliable scheduling, and sharing that does not require a training course.

Today more than 6,000 teams use Quillmark every week. The median team has 11 people in it, which is roughly what we imagined when we sketched the product on a whiteboard.

## What we got wrong

**Pricing, at first.** When we launched in 2023, Team cost $19 per user per month, and we had no free plan at all. That price did not cover the support load of the teams that loved us most, and the lack of a free plan meant people could not try Quillmark with their colleagues before asking a manager for a card. We added Free in 2024 and moved Team to the price it has today in January 2025. A handful of customers were upset, and we honored their old price for a full year. I would make the same call again, but I would explain it better.

**Permissions.** We treated access control as an enterprise feature for too long. Row-level security should have shipped two years before it did. It is now on Team as well as Business, where it belongs.

**Mobile.** We still do not have a good mobile experience. That is on the roadmap for this year, and I will say more when there is something to show.

## What comes next

We are hiring in support and in data engineering, and we are opening a small office in Lisbeth Harbor this autumn. We are also working on a much better alerting system, which is by far the most requested feature on our public board.

Thank you for three good years. See you at year four.

Ravi
